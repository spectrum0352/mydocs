# Signs of a Top-Performing Security Engineer
## How to Accelerate Your Security Engineering Career in Oil & Energy Companies

Technical skills are only part of what makes a successful security engineer.

The highest-performing security professionals consistently demonstrate ownership, clear communication, strategic thinking, and the ability to influence others across the organization.

Whether you work in cloud security, OT/ICS security, SOC operations, identity, governance, or security architecture, these seven habits can help you become a trusted security partner rather than just a technical expert.

---

# 1. Act with Autonomy

Great security engineers don't wait for instructions.

They identify risks, investigate problems, and proactively recommend solutions before someone asks.

Instead of saying:

> "Nobody assigned this."

They say:

> "I noticed a security gap, investigated it, and here's my recommendation."

### Why it matters

Security teams often support multiple business units simultaneously. Managers value engineers who can work independently while keeping stakeholders informed.

### How to apply it

- Identify security gaps before audits find them.
- Take ownership of incidents until resolution.
- Improve existing processes without waiting for approval.
- Automate repetitive security tasks.
- Document solutions for future teams.

### Example

Rather than waiting for a vulnerability review meeting, create a remediation plan and share it with application owners.

---

# 2. Get Things Done

Security work often stalls because of uncertainty, excessive analysis, or competing priorities.

Top performers maintain momentum.

They focus on delivering value instead of chasing perfection.

### Why it matters

Delayed security improvements increase organizational risk.

Progress reduces exposure.

### How to apply it

- Break large security projects into smaller milestones.
- Remove blockers early.
- Escalate issues when needed.
- Make informed decisions using available data.
- Deliver incremental improvements.

### Example

Instead of waiting for a perfect Zero Trust design, deploy MFA, Conditional Access, and Privileged Identity Management first, then expand the architecture over time.

---

# 3. Prioritize Ruthlessly

Everything cannot be a Priority 1.

Successful security engineers understand business risk and focus on activities that provide the greatest reduction in risk.

### Why it matters

Time, budget, and engineering resources are always limited.

Prioritization ensures effort is spent where it delivers the highest value.

### How to apply it

Prioritize work such as:

- Critical vulnerabilities before low-risk findings
- Internet-facing systems before internal assets
- Identity protection before cosmetic improvements
- High-value business applications before non-critical systems

### Example

Remediating an exposed production identity system should take precedence over updating internal documentation.

---

# 4. Communicate Clearly

Security is difficult enough without confusing communication.

Top-performing engineers explain complex technical concepts in language appropriate for their audience.

### Why it matters

Clear communication accelerates decision-making and builds trust across technical and business teams.

### How to apply it

Write communications that answer:

- What happened?
- Why does it matter?
- What is the business impact?
- What are the options?
- What action is required?
- Who owns the next step?

### Good communication includes

- Clear emails
- Concise meeting summaries
- Well-documented architecture
- Actionable risk reports
- Executive-friendly presentations

---

# 5. Build Strong Relationships

Security is a team sport.

Most security initiatives require collaboration across multiple departments.

Examples include:

- Cloud Engineering
- Infrastructure
- Network Teams
- Identity Teams
- Application Development
- OT/ICS Engineering
- Risk and Compliance
- Operations
- Executive Leadership

Relationships built before a crisis make incident response significantly easier.

### Why it matters

People support security initiatives when they trust the individuals behind them.

### How to apply it

- Understand business priorities.
- Listen before recommending controls.
- Respect operational constraints.
- Share knowledge openly.
- Celebrate team successes.

In oil and energy organizations, strong relationships between IT, OT, engineering, and operations are essential for maintaining secure and reliable critical infrastructure.

---

# 6. Maintain a Positive Attitude

Cybersecurity is full of incidents, vulnerabilities, changing threats, and unexpected challenges.

Top performers remain calm under pressure.

They focus on solving problems rather than assigning blame.

### Why it matters

Positive professionals improve team morale and encourage better collaboration during stressful situations.

### How to apply it

- Stay solution-focused.
- Learn from failures.
- Support teammates during incidents.
- Treat setbacks as learning opportunities.
- Encourage continuous improvement.

A positive attitude does not ignore problems—it helps teams overcome them.

---

# 7. Think Strategically

Great security engineers don't just solve today's problem.

They build solutions that support the organization's long-term objectives.

### Why it matters

Security investments should enable business growth while reducing risk.

Strategic thinking helps balance:

- Security
- Cost
- Operational efficiency
- Compliance
- Business agility

### How to apply it

Ask questions such as:

- Will this scale in three years?
- Does this align with Zero Trust principles?
- Can this process be automated?
- Will this reduce operational overhead?
- Does this support business objectives?

### Example

Instead of creating another manual approval process, implement automation using Microsoft Entra, Microsoft Defender, Microsoft Sentinel, Azure Policy, or Power Automate where appropriate.

---

# Applying These Habits in Oil & Energy Companies

Security engineers supporting oil, gas, and energy organizations operate in complex environments that combine enterprise IT with Operational Technology (OT) and Industrial Control Systems (ICS).

Success requires balancing cybersecurity with safety, operational reliability, and regulatory compliance.

High-performing security engineers:

- Build trust with plant engineers and operations teams.
- Understand the impact of downtime on critical infrastructure.
- Prioritize risks that affect production and safety.
- Communicate clearly with technical and non-technical stakeholders.
- Support compliance while enabling business operations.
- Automate repetitive security tasks where appropriate.
- Align security initiatives with business and operational goals.

These behaviors help create resilient, secure, and reliable environments for critical infrastructure.

---

# Key Takeaways

Top-performing security engineers consistently:

- Take ownership without waiting for direction.
- Deliver results and maintain momentum.
- Focus on high-value work.
- Communicate with clarity.
- Build trusted relationships.
- Stay positive during challenges.
- Think strategically about long-term outcomes.

Technical expertise opens the door.

Professional habits, leadership, and collaboration determine long-term career growth.

---

## Final Thoughts

The best security professionals are not simply experts in technology—they are trusted advisors who help organizations make better decisions.

Whether you're a Security Engineer, Cloud Security Architect, SOC Analyst, Security Consultant, or Security Leader, developing these seven habits will strengthen your impact, improve collaboration, and accelerate your career.

Continuous learning, ownership, and effective communication are what separate good security engineers from exceptional ones.
