# Post-Incident Review (PIR) Questionnaire

Section 1: Timeline & Detection
When did the incident start, and when did we first detect it? (Calculates "Dwell Time").
What was the "Smoking Gun"? (Which tool or person first raised the alarm?)
Were there precursors we missed? (Looking back, were there smaller alerts days or weeks ago that hinted at this?)
 

Section 2: Response & Process
Did the Incident Response Plan work? (Was the documentation helpful, or did the team have to "wing it"?)
Were roles and responsibilities clear? (Did everyone know who was in charge of communication vs. technical fixes?)
Were the right tools available? (Did the team have the forensic or administrative access they needed immediately?)
 

Section 3: Technical Root Cause
How did the attacker bypass our current defense? (Was it a technical failure, a human error, or a process gap?)
What was the "Blast Radius"? (Did the incident spread further than it should have? If so, why?)
 

Section 4: Communication
How effective was internal communication? (Was management kept informed without distracting the technical team?)
Was external communication handled correctly? (Did we meet legal notification deadlines for customers or regulators?)
 

Section 5: Future Prevention
What is the #1 thing we can change to prevent this exact incident from happening again?
Are there other systems in our environment with the same vulnerability?
 

 

 

How to use this?
 

·       Timing: Hold this meeting within 48–72 hours of the incident closure while details are fresh.

·       Atmosphere: Keep it "Blame-Free." The goal is to fix the system, not punish the person.

·       Output: The answers to these questions should be distilled into a Final Incident Report and used to update your IR Plan.
