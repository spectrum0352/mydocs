
Information Security (InfoSec) is the practice of protecting information by managing and reducing information-related risks. It is a key component of information risk management and focuses on safeguarding information from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification, destruction, inspection, recording, or devaluation.

The goal of information security is not only to prevent security incidents but also to minimize their impact when they occur.

Goals of Security

The core objectives of information security are represented by the CIA Triad. Security controls, technologies, and practices are designed to support one or more of these objectives.

The CIA Triad
1. Confidentiality

Confidentiality ensures that information and systems are accessible only to authorized individuals. It prevents unauthorized access, viewing, disclosure, or exposure of sensitive data.

2. Integrity

Integrity ensures that data remains accurate, complete, and trustworthy. It protects information from unauthorized modification or deletion and ensures that any unauthorized changes can be detected and corrected.

3. Availability

Availability ensures that systems, applications, data, and security services are operational and accessible when needed by authorized users. This includes maintaining reliable access channels and authentication mechanisms.

Note: Additional security principles such as authenticity, accountability, non-repudiation, and reliability are also important components of a comprehensive security framework (ISO/IEC 27000:2009).

Auditing and Accountability

Auditing and accountability involve tracking and recording user and system activities. This includes monitoring:

Who accessed a system or resource
When access occurred
What actions were performed
Which data was accessed or modified

These records help organizations investigate incidents, ensure compliance, and maintain accountability.

Non-Repudiation

Non-repudiation is the assurance that an individual or entity cannot deny the authenticity or validity of an action, communication, or transaction.

In information security, non-repudiation provides evidence of:

The origin of data
The integrity of data
The identity of the sender or participant in a transaction

This concept is widely used in legal, regulatory, and security contexts to ensure accountability and trust in digital communications and transactions.
